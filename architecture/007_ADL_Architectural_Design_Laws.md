# ADL-001
# Ari Architectural Design Laws

Version: 1.0

Status: Active

Purpose

The Ari Design Laws define the immutable engineering principles of Ari.

These laws exist above implementation.

Programming languages may change.

Models may change.

Databases may change.

The Design Laws do not.

---

LAW 1

One Responsibility

Every cognitive system has exactly one primary responsibility.

No module should perform unrelated cognitive work.

---

LAW 2

Understanding Before Action

No cognitive system should perform work until understanding has been established.

---

LAW 3

Evidence Before Belief

No permanent belief may exist without supporting evidence.

---

LAW 4

Relationships Before Duplication

Knowledge should connect.

Not duplicate.

---

LAW 5

Truth Before Confidence

Confidence never overrides evidence.

---

LAW 6

Reflection Before Completion

High-impact reasoning should always be reflected upon before communication.

---

LAW 7

Memory Has Purpose

Memory exists only when future value exists.

---

LAW 8

Learning Requires Review

No permanent learning occurs automatically.

---

LAW 9

Communication Is The Final Step

Speaking is always the last cognitive activity.

---

LAW 10

Architecture Above Implementation

Implementation serves architecture.

Architecture never serves implementation.

---

LAW 11

Replaceability

Every major component should be replaceable.

Language models

Search providers

Databases

Vector stores

UI

Everything except Ari's cognitive architecture.

---

LAW 12

Observability

Every important cognitive decision should be inspectable.

The Founder should always understand why Ari behaved the way she did.

---

LAW 13

Measurability

Every cognitive capability should have measurable metrics.

If it cannot be measured,

it cannot be improved.

---

LAW 14

Simplicity

Prefer the simplest architecture capable of producing truthful understanding.

---

LAW 15

The Founder Law

Every major architectural decision should strengthen Ari's ability to:

Observe

Understand

Reason

Reflect

Learn

Teach

If it strengthens none of these,

it probably does not belong.
